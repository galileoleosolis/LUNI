1The problem, and what LUNI does about it

The problem (from NASA's challenge page)What LUNI does
Sun and Earth information exists, but it is spread across several expert toolsPuts it on one screen
Those tools give raw numbers that you have to interpretGives a plain GO or NO-GO, with the reason
A launch delay forces a new landing dateFinds the next good landing time, and shows 12 months of good landing days
A landing site may need to move a littleScans the ground nearby for a better spot and moves the site there
South-pole hills and craters block the Sun and the EarthUses the real skyline from NASA's elevation data
Comparing options takes a long timePin any site and date, then compare them side by side
Hard to use for people who are not specialistsPlain words everywhere, and you can ask by typing or talking

* Existing NASA tools: QuickMap, Moon Trek and the JPL Horizons System, the three named on the challenge page. That column reflects the challenge page's own description of those tools and our use of them. It is not a complete audit of every feature they have. LUNI's Sun and Earth positions come from NASA NAIF SPICE (DE440) and agree with JPL Horizons to within 0.0007°; its terrain comes from LRO LOLA elevation maps.
